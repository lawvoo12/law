Alabama Car Accident Law

Car Accident Laws in Alabama — What You Must Know

Alabama Contributory Negligence — Critical Warning

Alabama is one of only 4 states in the USA that follows the doctrine of pure contributory negligence. Unlike most states where you can recover compensation even if you are partially at fault, Alabama law says that if you are found even 1% responsible for the accident, you may be completely barred from recovering ANY compensation.

This is why having an experienced Alabama car accident attorney is absolutely essential. Insurance companies know this law — and they will try to find any way to assign even partial blame to you in order to avoid paying your claim.

Alabama Statute of Limitations — 2 Years

In Alabama, you have 2 years from the date of the car accident to file a personal injury lawsuit. Missing this deadline means permanently losing your right to compensation — regardless of how serious your injuries are. Do not delay — contact an Alabama car accident lawyer immediately.

Alabama Minimum Insurance Requirements

Alabama requires drivers to carry minimum liability insurance of $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $25,000 for property damage. However, many Alabama accidents involve uninsured or underinsured drivers. Your attorney can help you recover from multiple sources.

Alabama Car Accident Statistics

According to ALDOT, Alabama sees approximately 150,000 car crashes per year, resulting in over 900 fatalities and 28,000 injuries annually. Interstate 65, Highway 280, and Highway 431 are among the most dangerous roads in the state.

Car Accident Lawyer Alabama — FAQ

Common questions about car accident claims in Alabama.

What is Alabama's contributory negligence law?
Alabama is one of only 4 states with pure contributory negligence. If you are found even 1% at fault for the accident, you may be completely barred from recovering ANY compensation. This makes having an experienced Alabama car accident lawyer absolutely critical. Insurance companies use this law aggressively to deny claims.
How long do I have to file a car accident claim in Alabama?
Alabama has a 2-year statute of limitations for personal injury claims. You must file your lawsuit within 2 years of the accident date or lose your right to compensation forever. For claims against government entities (like a city bus or government vehicle), the deadline may be shorter. Contact a lawyer immediately.
What is the average car accident settlement in Alabama?
Car accident settlements in Alabama vary based on injury severity, medical bills, lost wages, and the specific circumstances. Minor injury cases may settle for $10,000-$30,000, while serious injury cases often result in $100,000 or more. Wrongful death cases can result in much larger awards. Contact us for a free case evaluation.
Does Alabama require car insurance?
Yes — Alabama requires minimum liability insurance of $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident for bodily injury and $25,000 for property damage. However, many Alabama drivers are uninsured or underinsured. Your attorney can help identify all available sources of compensation.
What are the most dangerous roads in Alabama for car accidents?
According to Alabama Department of Transportation data, the most dangerous roads include Interstate 65, Highway 280 in Birmingham, Highway 431 in Anniston, and US 231. Mobile, Montgomery, and Birmingham counties have the highest number of car crashes in the state.
How much does a car accident lawyer cost in Alabama?
Alabama car accident lawyers work on contingency — they only get paid if you win. The typical fee is 33% of the settlement. You pay nothing upfront. Given Alabama's harsh contributory negligence law, having a lawyer is even more important here than in other states.
What should I do immediately after a car accident in Alabama?
Call 911 immediately. Get medical treatment. Take photos. Get witness information. Do NOT admit fault or apologize — this is especially critical in Alabama due to contributory negligence. Do not give a recorded statement to any insurance company without first consulting an Alabama car accident attorney.
Can I recover compensation if the other driver was uninsured in Alabama?
Yes — you can potentially recover through your own uninsured motorist (UM) coverage if you have it, or by filing a lawsuit directly against the at-fault driver. An Alabama car accident attorney can advise you on all available options in your specific situation.
