North Carolina Fault Law

Contributory Negligence — even 1% fault may bar all recovery.

Filing Deadline in North Carolina

You have 3 years from the date of the incident to file a claim in North Carolina. Missing this deadline means permanently losing your right to compensation.

North Carolina Insurance Requirements

North Carolina requires minimum liability insurance of $30,000/$60,000. Many drivers are uninsured — your attorney can identify all available compensation sources.

Most Dangerous Roads in North Carolina

The most dangerous roads in North Carolina include I-85, I-40, I-77, US 74. These corridors account for a significant percentage of serious injury accidents in the state.

Pedestrian Right-of-Way Laws in North Carolina

Pedestrian accident claims in North Carolina often involve disputes over right-of-way, especially at crosswalks and intersections. While pedestrians generally have the right-of-way in marked crosswalks, drivers making turns, failing to yield, or driving distracted are common causes of these serious collisions. Because pedestrians have virtually no protection in a crash with a vehicle, injuries are often severe, including fractures, traumatic brain injury, and spinal cord damage, making thorough documentation of the scene and traffic signals critical to establishing fault.
