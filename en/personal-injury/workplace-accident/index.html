About Workplace Accident Law

Workplace Accident Lawyer vs Work Injury Attorney — What You Need to Know

Whether you call it a workplace accident, work injury, on the job injury, occupational accident, or job site injury — if you were hurt while working due to unsafe conditions or employer negligence, you may be entitled to both workers compensation AND a personal injury lawsuit.

Many injured workers don't realize they may have two separate claims — a workers compensation claim through their employer AND a personal injury lawsuit against a third party (like an equipment manufacturer or property owner). An experienced workplace accident attorney can help you maximize your total recovery.

Workers Compensation vs Workplace Accident Lawsuit

Workers compensation provides quick but limited benefits — covering medical bills and partial lost wages. A personal injury lawsuit against a third party can recover much more — full lost wages, pain and suffering, and other damages not covered by workers comp. Our attorneys evaluate both options and fight for maximum compensation. You pay nothing unless we win.

FAQ

Workplace Accident Lawyer — Work Injury & On the Job Injury Attorney USA FAQ

Everything you need to know about Workplace Accident claims in the USA.

What is the difference between a workplace accident lawyer and a work injury attorney?
They are the same — workplace accident lawyer, work injury attorney, on the job injury lawyer, and occupational accident attorney all handle cases where someone was injured at work or on a job site.
Can I sue my employer for a workplace injury?
In most states, workers compensation is the exclusive remedy against your employer. However, you may be able to sue third parties — equipment manufacturers, property owners, contractors — whose negligence contributed to your injury. A workplace accident attorney can identify all liable parties.
Should I file workers comp AND a personal injury lawsuit?
Possibly — many workplace injury victims are entitled to both. Workers comp provides quick but limited benefits. A personal injury lawsuit against a third party can recover pain and suffering and other damages not covered by workers comp. Contact a work injury attorney to evaluate both options.
How much is a workplace accident case worth?
It depends on your injuries and the circumstances. Workers comp typically pays 2/3 of wages plus medical bills. A third-party lawsuit can recover full damages including pain and suffering — often totaling $60,000 to $500,000 or more.
How long do I have to report a workplace injury?
You should report your injury to your employer immediately. Workers comp claims have very short filing deadlines — sometimes as little as 30 days in some states. Personal injury lawsuits have a 2-3 year statute of limitations. Contact a workplace accident attorney immediately.
